# Relevance of Pan-Genome Metrics

Pan-genome metrics—core, soft-core, shell, and cloud gene counts—are central to interpreting the genetic diversity and evolutionary flexibility of the 13 *Enterobacterales* genomes analyzed in this project. The Panaroo results reveal a total pan-genome of 7,086 gene families, of which 3,606 (50.9%) are core genes present across all or nearly all strains, 1,846 (26.1%) are shell genes shared among a subset of strains, and 1,634 (23.1%) are cloud genes found in only one or a few isolates. These proportions directly reflect the "openness" of the pan-genome: a large shell and cloud partition relative to the core indicates that the collective genome continues to expand as new isolates are included, consistent with the known genomic plasticity of Enterobacterales driven by horizontal gene transfer, mobile genetic elements, and niche-specific adaptations. The core genome size establishes a conserved functional backbone useful for phylogenetic reconstruction and identifying broadly conserved drug targets, while the accessory genome (shell + cloud) captures strain-specific traits such as virulence factors, antibiotic resistance genes, and metabolic versatility that differentiate individual isolates. Comparing these partition sizes across the four tools used here—Roary, Panaroo, PPanGGOLiN, and ggCaller—also serves a methodological purpose: discrepancies in core vs. accessory boundaries between tools expose algorithmic differences in gene clustering, error correction, and graph construction, helping evaluate which approach is most appropriate for downstream analyses of this particular taxon.
