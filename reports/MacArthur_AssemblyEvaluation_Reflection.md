1. Relevance of Metrics
To evaluate the assemblies, I used QUAST to generate physical contiguity and correctness metrics (N50, total length, contig count, and ambiguous base counts) and BUSCO against the enterobacterales_odb10 dataset to measure biological completeness via single-copy ortholog presence. Given the context of Enterobacterales (typically ~4.5–5.5 Mbp circular chromosomes), both toolsets are essential to ensure the physical sequence structure is intact and the necessary functional gene space is actually present. To be considered a "good" assembly for downstream use, I looked for criteria satisfying the "3 C's": high contiguity (N50 > 1 Mbp and ideally < 10 total contigs), high completeness (> 95% complete BUSCOs), and high correctness (minimal ambiguous N's and < 1% duplicated BUSCOs to rule out artifactual haplotypes).

2. Assembly Evaluation Results
The evaluation revealed a stark bimodal distribution in quality across the dataset concerning Contiguity, Completeness, and Correctness. Seven genomes performed exceptionally well, achieving "platinum" status with massive N50s (>3 Mbp), assembling into just 1 to 5 contigs, and scoring >98% in BUSCO completeness while maintaining high correctness via low duplication rates (<0.5%). Conversely, four genomes (ASM17127v1, ASM17025v1, ASM17131v1, and ASM17125v1) showed remarkably poor assemblies, suffering from severe physical fragmentation (ranging from 19 to 113 contigs with N50s well under 500 kbp) and unacceptably low gene completeness (~89-90%). Because missing ~10% of core orthologs implies these assemblies are fundamentally flawed, these four specific genomes should be strictly excluded from downstream comparative or phylogenetic analyses to prevent false-negative biological conclusions.
